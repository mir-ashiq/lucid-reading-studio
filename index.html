Reading that adapts to you, not the other way around.

One in five readers struggles with dyslexia — and those hit hardest are families without money for tutors or specialist software. Lucid rebuilds the reading experience itself: focus-line tracking, syllable and phoneme scaffolds, visual-fatigue veils, and a spelling trainer that grows from the words you tapped. Free forever, no account, no tracking, runs offline — built for classrooms, libraries and families, not just hackers.

    How Lucid helps

    Syllable tint — long words are split and tinted so the eye chunks instead of guessing.

    Focus line — one clear line at a time; the rest fades. Use ▲▼ or the auto timer.

    Fatigue veil — hides upcoming lines to cut visual noise, a common trigger for losing your place.

    Word workshop — tap any hard word: syllables, sounds, simple meaning, then a spaced spelling drill.

    Honest tech — every rule is transparent JavaScript, not a black-box AI. No data leaves your browser.

    Who it's for

    The 1 in 5 readers with dyslexic traits, ESL learners facing long English words, and any tired reader who loses their place in dense text. No diagnosis required — if reading is hard, it's for you.

    Where it works

    Classrooms, libraries and homes — anywhere a browser runs. It's one HTML file: save it, email it, put it on a USB stick. After the first load it works with the Wi-Fi off.

    Why it's free

    There is no server, no API key and no account — nothing to bill. A reading tool shouldn't vanish when a trial ends or a company pivots. Free forever is a design decision, not a promo.
